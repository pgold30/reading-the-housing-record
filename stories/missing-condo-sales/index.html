Condominium linkage: correction and release notes

An incorrect lot-number limit in my code excluded valid candidate units. The earlier Bronx coverage gap should not be interpreted as missing public records.

By Pablo Loschi1 October 2026Corrected 21 October; updated 22 October 2026Correction noticeSources & methods

What was wrong

The earlier version of this article presented the low Bronx condominium linkage rate as a limitation of the public records. My code searched unit lots only from 1001 to 1999. The city's documented condominium unit-lot range extends through 6999. That restriction excluded valid candidate units and explains much of the reported gap.

The original interpretation is withdrawn. Dataset 1.2.0 corrects the lot limit and rebuilds the affected links and mortgage indicators. The old coverage figures describe release 1.1.0 of my dataset; they do not establish that half the Bronx's condo deeds are missing from public records.

What the correction changes

Under the corrected rules, 4,261 of 4,311 eligible Bronx condominium sales link to a deed: 98.8%, compared with 51.7% before. Across the four ACRIS boroughs, coverage rises from 89.8% to 96.0%. The sales extract and house-link rows are unchanged.

The explorer now uses dataset 1.2.0. These are rule-based matches. Coverage does not establish accuracy against complete documents or show whether a mortgage financed the purchase. Corrected House 3.10 and Tax 3.3 PDFs and replication packages are available on their House and Tax pages. Earlier Zenodo deposits remain linked separately.

Sources and correction history

The official PLUTO dictionary, printed pages 2–3, describes condominium unit lots 1001–6999. The original article used dataset release 1.1.0. Its figures should not be used for the former borough-gap interpretation. Read the correction notice or check the dataset status.
