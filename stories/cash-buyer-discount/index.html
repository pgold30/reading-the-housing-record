The cash-buyer discount shrinks by half when likely family sales are left out

In New York City records, houses bought for cash sell for about 9% less. Leave out sales where buyer and seller share a surname and the gap halves.

By Pablo Loschi1 October 2026Updated 26 October 20264 min readSources & methods

A house sells twice: once to a buyer with a mortgage, once to a buyer who pays cash. In New York City’s records the cash buyer pays about 9% less. In May 2026 Albany weighed a 1% tax on all-cash purchases of $1 million or more. So who gets the cash discount, and how much of it is a reward for a sure closing?

What this means for a buyer

The measured cash gap is not a discount to apply to your offer. It changes with the transactions included, and a missing mortgage match does not prove that someone paid cash.

The financing label alone cannot answer that. The cash group includes company buyers and possible transfers between relatives, and it can include purchases whose mortgages the matching rule missed.

Comparing New York City houses that sold twice between 2016 and 2025, I find the financed sale priced 9.25 log points above the cash one. Put the other way round, cash buyers paid about 8.8% less.

Then I set aside the 358 sale pairs involving a deed where buyer and seller share a surname. The gap falls to 4.87 log points, about half.

Who is in the cash group?

A shared surname can point to a transfer between relatives, but unrelated people can share a name and relatives can have different ones. So halving the estimate does not by itself show how much of the gap comes from family sales.

The forms filed with the deeds back the screen up. In a random sample of 60 deeds, 16 of the 40 same-surname deeds declared a sale between relatives or showed another non-market sign, against 2 of the 20 other cash deeds. The reports were read with AI assistance and then checked against the forms by me. The sample is too small to say how often the screen gets it wrong. One of the 60 was a $1.6 million sale within a family.

Company buyers look different. When the cash buyer is a company, the gap is 1.53 log points, with a 95% interval from minus 1.79 to plus 5.44. These records cannot pin down a positive gap for that group.

Some of those companies are individuals buying through an LLC. The name says little about their money or how sure their offer was, and it does not make them large institutional investors.

Because the comparison uses the same property twice, it holds fixed whatever does not change about a house. It cannot catch everything that does, such as renovations or the conditions of a particular sale.

Finding a mortgage is a separate problem

The financing label comes from matching each sale to mortgages recorded against the same parcel around the sale date. A nearby mortgage only counts as purchase financing when its borrower, collateral and purpose fit the purchase.

A missing match proves little either. A loan can cover more than one lot, carry an unexpected date or slip past the rule. When 53 sale pairs were reviewed, four of the 50 sampled cash labels turned out to have a same-day purchase mortgage: two because the city's index had the date a year off, two because the loan was on an adjoining lot.

How much the result depends on errors

Dropping the four pairs with documented label errors leaves the main estimate at 9.40 log points. The paper also simulates labelling errors in both directions across the full sample; random errors shrink the gap without reversing it. Because the reviewed pairs mix random and targeted cases, their error counts are not a rate for the whole sample.

Errors that happened to fall on the pairs working against the result could reverse it, and the document review is too small to rule that out. With the documented corrections and the surname screen applied together, the estimate is 5.19 log points, with a 95% interval of 2.98 to 7.18.

A targeted follow-up turned up three bundle prices covering several parcels and four mortgages whose index dates contradict the documents themselves. Applying rules for those problems across the sample gives 9.91 log points, a sensitivity check that depends on those rules. One document a deed referred to turned out to be a power of attorney.

Before using a cash discount to judge an offer or argue for a tax, check which transactions went into it. The gap changes substantially when possible family transfers are removed. These records leave the value of closing certainty unresolved.
