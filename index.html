Why public housing records mislead

Three places where law and recordkeeping shape the numbers

Before taking a public price series, cash-buyer discount, or assessment roll at face value, researchers have to read the tax and registry rules behind each column.

Tax statutes sit inside recorded prices

Round-price transfer-tax cliffs move deals from $2 million to $1,999,999, foreclosure deeds record judgment debt instead of bids, and Class 2 rental proxies value $4–$6 million condos near $1 million.

Unlinked records are not cash or exempt sales

Condominium sales often carry building billing lots while deeds sit on unit lots, leaving expensive units disproportionately unlinked. Treating an empty recording window as cash distorts price-gap estimates.

An open NYC benchmark after ZTRAX

With Zillow’s direct ZTRAX servicing wound down in 2023, researchers lack a current, NDA-free linked record for New York City. This deposit provides documented unit-lot links through 2025 under CC BY 4.0.

The data · Post-ZTRAX NYC benchmark

Sales linked to deeds and mortgages (2016–2025)

The public release retains 729,047 residential sales rows across all five boroughs and links 305,175 eligible house (97.2%) and condominium (96.0%) sales in the four ACRIS boroughs to recorded deeds and mortgage indicators.

Zero NDA or gatekeeper

CC BY 4.0 tables plus standard-library Python code that rebuilds the links offline from archived NYC Open Data extracts and manifests.

Current through 2025

Covers ten annual cohorts including the post-2021 rate shift, after Zillow ended direct servicing of ZTRAX in September 2023.

Explicit condo unit-lot rules

Recovers condominium unit lots (1001–6999) with documented coverage and caveats instead of an opaque vendor flag.

About

Pablo Loschi

Lawyer (UBA) · Executive MBA in progress (Quantic)
Principal Systems Engineer · Independent researcher · Berlin

Pablo Loschi is a Principal Systems Engineer at Verve in Berlin and an independent researcher in housing economics and tax design. After more than fifteen years building large-scale cloud and data platforms, first in Argentina and for the past seven years in Germany, he brings an engineer's habits to empirical work: documented linkage rules, versioned releases and replication code. The papers distinguish agreement between files from verified accuracy. Trained as a lawyer at the University of Buenos Aires, he reads the statutes behind the records, and each of these papers starts from one.

Housing finance is also a practical interest. He founded HolaCasaClara, a free, independent comparator of Argentine mortgage loans that turns banks' fine print into total borrowing costs; its launch was covered by iProfesional and elDiarioAR, and Ámbito Financiero used it as a source.
