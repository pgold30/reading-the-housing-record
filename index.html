Reading the Housing Record

A deed amount, a financing label and a tax liability look like simple facts. Each comes from a legal or administrative process that was not designed for measurement. These papers ask what New York City's housing records actually measure, and how tax rules shape the transactions they describe.

Three papers, separate claims

The studies share a data setting but not a causal chain. Each has its own sample, estimand, uncertainty and validation requirements. Public versions and replication archives are linked on each page. New to the jargon? See key terms.

COMPANION THEORY · SIMULATION, NO RECORDS

The Execution-Certainty Wedge

Allocation, Distribution, and the Design of Housing-Market Instruments

The question: if a buyer's certainty of closing, typically a cash buyer's, can decide who gets a home, which policy tool widens access at the least cost? A stylised agent-based market compares acquisition caps, fair-share quotas, constant charges and contention-triggered duties.

What it finds: at full-run access targets, a constant charge preserves more buyer value than a signal-based duty while delivering slightly smaller access gains; the ranking shifts with how access is measured, how quotas are accounted and how welfare is weighted.

A conditional comparison, not a calibrated policy evaluation: it omits strategic bidding, renter welfare and administrative costs. The empirical papers motivate it but do not confirm its premise. Who Gets the House? finds that much of the observed cash discount reflects who pays cash rather than certainty itself.

The data

One public record system, three different slices

"Same data" means the papers draw on the same free New York City records, not that they share a sample or a result. Each builds its own sample and answers its own question.

Two public sources

Department of Finance sales fileEvery recorded property sale: price, date, address and building class.
ACRIS, the city's land registryEvery deed and mortgage recorded in Manhattan, the Bronx, Brooklyn and Queens: document type, amount, dates, the parcel it touches, and the names and addresses of the parties.

Linked record by record

Parcel (borough–block–lot) + date + amountA sale is matched to its own deed. A mortgage recorded on the same parcel within days of the deed marks the purchase as financed; none marks it as unfinanced. For condominiums the deed reveals the unit the sales file hides. Where the right answer is known, the match finds the correct parcel 99.6% of the time.

Three samples

The Shape of the TaxPrices and loan amounts: 172,633 financed purchases; 431,471 sales around the tax thresholds, 2016–2025. Who Gets the House?Repeat sales of the same property, with the deed's parties: 6,006 house pairs, 2016–2025. When a Deed Is Not a Market SaleEvery deed and its parties: 1.47 million deeds, 2003–2025, and 238,290 repeat-sale pairs.

All sources are free NYC Open Data, and each paper's archive records the exact extracts it used. The companion theory paper uses no records; it is a simulation. Staten Island deeds are kept by the county clerk, outside ACRIS. Co-operative apartments change hands as shares rather than deeds, so they are largely invisible to deed-based measurement.

Why it matters

What changes if the records are read carefully

Price indices

Registry amounts are not always prices. Lender take-title deeds pass ordinary screens and shift the timing of a repeat-sales index through the 2008 crisis; the fix is to classify deeds by their parties.

The cash discount

A "cash" label can mark who is buying—companies, relatives, lenders—rather than what closing certainty is worth. That makes an observed cash discount a weak basis for taxing or subsidising a payment method.

Tax design

What can be measured directly is statutory liability and how transactions move around thresholds. Those, not an unidentified price gap, are the objects a tax-design debate can rely on.

Key terms

Reading the papers without the jargon

Log points
100 × the difference in logarithms. For small values it is close to a percentage: 9.25 log points ≈ 9.7%.
Repeat sales
Comparing two sale prices of the same property, so fixed features of the home (size, location) cancel out.
Deed and consideration
A deed transfers ownership; the consideration is the amount recorded on it, usually but not always the price.
ACRIS
New York City's public land registry, where deeds and mortgages for four of the five boroughs are recorded.
Referee's deed · deed in lieu
Ways a lender takes title in foreclosure: at a court-ordered auction, or by the owner handing the property over.
Notch
A tax that jumps on the whole price once a threshold is crossed: a $999,999 sale owes no mansion tax, a $1,000,000 sale owes $10,000.
Bunching
Sales piling up just below a notch, and thinning out just above it.
Statutory vs economic incidence
Who the law says pays a tax, versus who ends up bearing its cost once prices and terms adjust.
Static costing
Revenue computed with transactions held fixed: the size of a change, not a forecast of how people respond.
Concept DOI
A permanent link that always resolves to the latest version of a paper on Zenodo.

Validation status

What is still being checked

Two independent human readers have yet to complete the blinded instrument review for the house paper (53 repeat-sale pairs) and the probability-sample review for the deed paper (400 deeds). Classification error, its uncertainty and any resulting reruns will be reported after those reviews. The current public versions should be read with those checks outstanding. Readings assisted by AI tools are not counted as independent human review.

The tax paper's statutory calculations and reform comparison do not depend on these document audits. Its revised draft is complete and will replace the public preprint on Zenodo.

Pablo Loschi is a Principal Systems Engineer at Verve in Berlin and an independent researcher in housing economics and tax design. After more than fifteen years building large-scale cloud and data platforms, first in Argentina and for the past seven years in Germany, he brings an engineer's habits to empirical work: pipelines that reconcile every record against its source, versioned releases, and replication archives that rebuild each number from public data. Trained as a lawyer at the University of Buenos Aires, he also reads the statutes behind the records, which is where each of these papers begins.

Housing finance is also a practical interest. He co-founded HolaCasaClara, a free, independent comparator of Argentine mortgage loans that turns banks' fine print into total borrowing costs; its launch was covered by iProfesional and elDiarioAR, and Ámbito Financiero used it as a source.
