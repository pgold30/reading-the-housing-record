Why these records need linking

New York City publishes its property sales and its recorded deeds and mortgages as separate open datasets, and the two do not join directly. The Department of Finance annualized and rolling sales files give the price and date but no ACRIS document number. ACRIS, the city register, holds the deeds and mortgages but not the sales. Condominiums are the hardest case: the sales file usually identifies a unit by the building’s billing lot, while ACRIS records its deed and mortgage under the unit’s own lot, so a direct parcel join misses most of them.

This dataset links the records under fixed, documented rules so outside researchers can reuse the same tables without proprietary vendor licenses. Public files contain no party names or mailing addresses.

An open, post-ZTRAX public-record benchmark for New York City

Following Zillow’s September 2023 wind-down of direct ZTRAX servicing, empirical housing researchers have relied on closed commercial files for linked deed and mortgage indicators. This deposit is scoped specifically to New York City rather than national parcel coverage, and rests on three transparent properties:

Zero NDA or institutional gatekeeper

CC BY 4.0 CSV tables alongside standard-library Python code that rebuilds sales and links offline from archived public extracts and manifests.

Current coverage through 2025

Spans ten full annual cohorts (2016–2025), covering the post-2021 higher-rate housing market after the ZTRAX direct-servicing sunset.

Explicit unit-lot linkage (1001–6999)

Documents the exact three-rule condominium recovery logic, borough and price-band coverage, and unlinked financing missingness instead of an opaque vendor flag.

In numbers

  • 729,047house, condominium, and co-operative sales retained (2016–2025, all five boroughs, including 213,457 zero-price transfers)
  • 97.2%of 189,279 eligible house sales linked to a deed in the four ACRIS boroughs (183,912 links)
  • 96.0%of 126,251 eligible condominium sales linked to a deed (121,263 links; falls to 71.6% at $6M+)
  • 99.7% / 99.0%street-address agreement (1,993 of 1,999 comparable links) and condo-unit agreement (1,432 of 1,446) in a 2,000-link stratified sample

A link identifies a candidate document under the stated rules; it does not establish accuracy against unverified source images or confirm that a recorded mortgage financed the purchase.

Files in the deposited 1.2.0 dataset

Three compressed CSV tables share keys: sales joins one-to-one to links on sale_id, and links joins to deed_flags on deed_document_id (note that 722 deeds link to 1,563 sales rows). Blank financing cells for unlinked condominiums mean unmeasured, never zero.

How the link works

  • Deed. A deed qualifies when its consideration lies within 0.5% of recorded sale price and its document date (falling back to recording date) falls within 45 days of sale. Houses match on their own parcel. Condominiums search within borough and block across unit lots 1001–6999 under three hierarchical rules, agreeing with the sales file’s own unit lot in 99.6% of single-parcel links (98,661 of 99,086). Bulk purchases failing documented guards remain unlinked (4,046 rows); where multiple qualifying deeds sit on one recovered parcel (946 of 305,175 links), the lowest document ID is retained.
  • Mortgage. A sale receives a recorded-mortgage indicator if a mortgage sits on its parcel (for condominiums, the recovered unit lot) from 15 days before to 30, 90, or 180 days after the sale. An indicator marks a filing in the window, not verified purchase debt.
  • Flags. Computed from ACRIS Parties records using documented string and surname rules. Because party names and frequent-surname tables are excluded from the public deposit, deed_flags.csv.gz is supplied as a fixed snapshot.

What has been checked

The archived extracts rebuild the sales and linkage tables offline, passing checks on join keys, document types, collateral parcels, amount tolerances, and date windows. Beyond structural checks, the companion linkage paper (Loschi, 2026) reports a stratified validation sample of 2,000 retained links (finding 99.7% building-address agreement and 99.0% condominium-unit agreement on unused ACRIS address records) alongside a blind research-assistant check of source deeds and mortgages for 40 house sales (with author review).

Limits

  • A mortgage indicator of 0 means no qualifying mortgage appeared in the search window; it is not proof of an all-cash purchase.
  • Co-operative apartments and Staten Island transfers are listed in sales.csv.gz but never linked: co-op share loans do not enter ACRIS real-property filings, and Staten Island deeds reside with the Richmond County Clerk.
  • Condominium deed coverage drops at high prices (71.6% at $6 million or more), leaving linked condominiums cheaper than unlinked sales (median $978,000 vs. $4 million). Users analyzing linked subsets should report coverage beside estimates.
  • Company, surname, address, and foreclosure-event flags are rule-based name screens with unmeasured population error rates.

Condominium coverage

Version 1.2.0 expanded the unit-lot search upper bound from 1999 to 6999, lifting condominium coverage to 94.7% in Manhattan, 98.8% in the Bronx, 97.7% in Brooklyn, and 96.1% in Queens. The earlier Bronx gap reflected that code boundary rather than missing public filings. Read the correction note.

Try the data

Run the small offline quickstart (1.47 MB) with standard-library Python to reproduce annual condominium coverage and the linked/unlinked price comparison without downloading the full archive. Or extract the complete 1.2.2 deposit (see the two-example guide) and run:

python3 examples/recorded_mortgage_example.py build --output annual_condo_coverage.csv
python3 examples/condo_selection.py build --output selection

If you test either script, email me what worked or where you got stuck.
