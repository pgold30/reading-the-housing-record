Where the units sit against the surcharge brackets

For condos and co-ops, the Phase 1 surcharge (July 2026 to June 2028) starts at a city market value of $1 million and applies to the whole value once a bracket is reached. It is not owed when the unit is the primary residence of the owner, the owner's spouse, child, parent, sibling, grandparent or grandchild, or a tenant on an arm's-length lease of at least one year (see the exemptions guide). The roll does not show residency, so these counts are not a list of who pays.

How the city valued the building

State law has DOF value condo units as if the building were a rental: it estimates the rent the building would earn, using comparable rental buildings, subtracts expenses and converts the net income into a value. That value is spread across the units. It is usually far below what the apartments sell for, which is why similar apartments in different buildings can land on opposite sides of the $1 million line. The rental-comparables guide explains the method.

In its latest published record (report year 2021), DOF valued the condominium section at $65.59 million, an implied capitalization rate of 12.3%, using 3 comparable rental buildings. The FY2027 Notice of Property Value lists the current figures.

The building summary lists DOF's income and expense figures and the comparable rental buildings it used, with units near each line and recorded sales. Or inspect a 2-page Sample Unit Report ($79 PDF).

Deadline to challenge the value

An owner who wants to contest the market value behind the surcharge files Tax Commission Form TC107 by March 1, 2027 for condos and co-ops. The form cannot be used to protest the assessed value. The TC107 guide covers the grounds, the filing steps and the separate timing for a residency appeal.
