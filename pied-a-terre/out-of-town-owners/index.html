Pied-à-terre tax · Out-of-town owners

Own a New York City home but live somewhere else?

What the pied-à-terre surcharge means for owners in New Jersey, Connecticut, Florida, Canada, the UK and everywhere else outside the city.

After New York, the places searching most for the pied-à-terre tax in the past year were New Jersey, Washington DC, Connecticut and Massachusetts, followed by Florida, Pennsylvania and Virginia. Outside the United States, interest came mostly from Canada and the United Kingdom. Owners who live elsewhere are the people the surcharge is aimed at, and the ones most likely to miss a notice.

Does it apply to you?

If you own a New York City condo, co-op apartment or one- to three-family house that is not your primary residence, it can. It applies when the city market value is over the line and no one who counts under the law lives there as a primary residence.

  • Condos and co-ops: from a city market value of $1 million. 4% of the whole value up to $3 million, 5.25% up to $5 million, 6.5% above.
  • One- to three-family houses: from $5 million. 0.8% up to $15 million, 1.05% up to $25 million, 1.3% above.
  • Period: Phase 1 runs from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2028. From July 1, 2028, condos and co-ops are valued from comparable sales and move to the houses' $5 million threshold and rates.

The surcharge is not owed when the home is the primary residence of the owner, the owner's spouse, child, parent, sibling, grandparent or grandchild, or a tenant or subtenant on a bona fide arm's-length lease of at least one year. Residence is tested on the taxable status date before the fiscal year: January 5, 2026 for 2026–27. When an LLC, corporation or partnership owns the home, the people who together hold a majority interest count as owners; when a trust owns it, its sole beneficiaries do. Owning through an entity does not by itself make a home anyone's primary residence. Other exemptions and abatements do not reduce the surcharge (Tax Law § 1354(a)).

What to check from out of town

  • Your mailing address. Notices and statements go to the address on the Department of Finance record. If it is the apartment itself or an old address, you may not have seen them. Statements and notices can be viewed by address or BBL on the Department of Finance website.
  • The city's value, not the price. Condos and co-ops are valued as if they were rentals, usually far below what they sell for. Look up the unit in the checker or its building in the building index.
  • Who lives there. A family member who lives in the home as a primary residence, or a tenant on a lease of a year or more, takes it out of the surcharge (see exemption rules).
  • The dates. The first bills are due January 1, 2027. The deadline to contest the market value is March 1, 2027 for condos and co-ops and March 15, 2027 for houses.

Deadline to appeal the value or residency

To contest the market value behind the surcharge, file Tax Commission Form TC107 by March 1, 2027 for condos and co-ops, or March 15, 2027 for houses. The same form appeals the Department of Finance's residency determination; if you first asked DOF to review residency, the deadline for that part is the March date or 30 days after DOF's final notice, whichever is later. The form cannot be used to protest the assessed value. See the TC107 guide or the deadline timeline.
