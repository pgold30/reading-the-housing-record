Paper 03 · Tax design

The Shape of the Tax

Mortgage Recording Liability and Transaction Shifting at New York City's 2019 Transfer-Tax Thresholds

By Pablo LoschiPublic preprint version 1.0Revised draft complete

The question

New York City charges borrowers a mortgage recording tax of 1.80–1.925% of the loan; a purchase without a recorded mortgage pays none of it. How large is that statutory charge on the loans people actually take, and did sales shift at the buyer-paid transfer-tax thresholds created in 2019?

The paper applies the published schedule to each observed loan and compares prices at the same thresholds before and after the reform, with the unchanged $1 million threshold as a control.

Key results

  • 1.41%of price for houses and 1.28% for condominiums: the borrower-side statutory charge on 172,633 observed loans
  • 7.8 → 1.6sales at exactly $2 million relative to local density, before and after it became a taxed threshold
  • $246Ma year: static cost of a four-borough residential purchase-money carve-out, transactions held fixed, condominium loans imputed

These are statutory liabilities and fixed-transaction costings. They do not identify who ultimately bears the tax, how sales would respond to a continuous tax, or an optimal rate.

Why it matters

  • Debates over taxing cash purchases rarely start from the fact that the code already charges the financed buyer. That statutory difference can be measured directly from loans, without relying on a contested price gap.
  • Even a quarter-point notch relocates transactions, and responses did not rise in proportion to the rate: the shape of a schedule matters, not only its level.
  • A fixed-transaction costing gives legislators the order of magnitude of a purchase-money carve-out and of continuous schedules that match it within the sample.
